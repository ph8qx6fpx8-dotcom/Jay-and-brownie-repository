# Paid Advertising Plan — Sophia Martin Jewelry

**Brand:** Sophia Martin Jewelry — sophiamartinjewelry.com
**Tagline:** "Heirlooms Redefined" · **Hero:** "Vintage Heirlooms, Beautifully Reimagined"
**Positioning:** High-end, largely one-of-a-kind vintage & Art Deco-revival fine jewelry. Genuine scarcity. Considered, higher-AOV purchase.
**Price ladder:** $300 (Art Deco Crystal & Green Glass Pendant) → $1,000 (Diamond & Blue Topaz Teardrop) → $1,500 (Onyx & Ruby Fringe) → $2,000 (18K White Gold Diamond & Topaz; Pearl & Multi-Gemstone Fringe) → $3,500 (Diamond Starburst Lariat). AOV assumed $300–$3,500+.
**Audience:** Women ~30–60, disposable income, vintage/Art Deco/antique lovers, collectors, heirloom gifting & self-purchase.
**Voice:** Elegant, warm, heritage-driven, luxury. Never discount-screamy.

> **Document status:** Strategic starting plan. Every benchmark, budget split, and ROAS target below is an **illustrative estimate** flagged with ⚠️. Replace each with the brand's own observed numbers within the first 30–60 days of spend. Nothing here should be treated as a guaranteed outcome.

## 1. Paid Objectives & Role of Paid vs. Organic/PR

### Primary objectives (in priority order)
1. **Profitable new-customer acquisition** at a healthy blended ROAS given high AOV.
2. **Build a warm audience / retargeting pool** (site visitors, video viewers, engagers) that organic + email + PR can keep nurturing — paid's biggest early job is to *fill the top and middle of the funnel*, not to force same-session purchases.
3. **Brand-equity lift** — teach the market what "Sophia Martin" means (antiques reimagined, one-of-a-kind heirlooms) so that organic/PR/referral conversions rise over time.
4. **Recover high-intent abandoners** (view, add-to-cart, checkout) — the cheapest, highest-ROAS spend for a high-consideration product.

### How paid complements organic / PR
- **Organic (Instagram, Pinterest, email):** carries the storytelling depth, behind-the-scenes sourcing, founder voice, and community. Paid amplifies the *best-performing* organic pieces (whitelisted/boosted Reels) rather than inventing throwaway ad creative.
- **PR / editorial:** any press hit (magazine feature, stylist mention, gift-guide placement) becomes a **paid "as seen in" social-proof asset** and a branded-search defensive layer. Fund branded search so PR-driven demand doesn't leak to resellers or competitors bidding on the name.
- **Email/SMS:** paid's job ends at the add-to-cart / lead; email + SMS flows (abandoned cart, browse abandon, back-in-stock-adjacent "new arrivals") do the patient closing work that a scarce, considered purchase requires.

### Realistic expectations for a high-consideration, high-AOV, one-of-a-kind product
- **Long consideration windows.** A $2,000 heirloom is researched over days or weeks, often across devices. Expect **multi-touch, multi-day paths to purchase**; last-click and 7-day-click attribution will *understate* paid's true contribution. Judge paid on **blended** performance (total revenue ÷ total ad spend, i.e. MER), not just in-platform ROAS.
- **Lower volume, higher value.** You will see **few conversions per week** at lean budgets. Statistical significance on creative tests comes slowly — resist killing an ad after 2 purchases. Lean on **upper-funnel signals** (CTR, hook rate, ATC rate, cost-per-landing-page-view, cost-per-ATC) to make faster decisions.
- **Scarcity shapes the creative mandate.** Because most SKUs are one-of-a-kind, a winning ad **cannot be tied to a single unit** — that unit may sell before the ad scales. Creative must sell **the brand, the aesthetic, and the collection** ("a curated world of one-of-a-kind Art Deco heirlooms"), with specific pieces used as rotating illustrations. Build **collection-level** campaigns, not single-SKU campaigns, so a sold-out piece doesn't kill a winning ad.
- **Catalog hygiene matters more than usual.** With one-of-a-kind inventory, feed must sync availability fast so you never pay to send traffic to a sold piece. Use "only 1 available" as an *honest* urgency lever, not a fabricated one.
- **Patience on payback.** With high AOV and gifting/self-purchase seasonality, expect a **ramp of 60–90 days** before blended ROAS stabilizes. Early ROAS will look worse than steady-state because the retargeting pool and pixel learning are still building.

**Channel roles:**
- **Meta (Instagram + Facebook) — PRIMARY.** Best storytelling surface for a visual, aspirational, founder-led fine-jewelry brand; strongest retargeting toolkit; Reels for motion craftsmanship, Feed/Carousel for collection and detail. Instagram is the hero placement; Facebook Feed + Marketplace adds cheap reach among the 40–60 segment.
- **Pinterest Ads — STRONG SECONDARY.** Uniquely high-intent for "Art Deco jewelry," "vintage engagement ring," "antique jewelry," "old money aesthetic." Pinners plan purchases and collect — perfect for a considered heirloom buy. Lower CPMs, longer pin shelf life.
- **Google (Shopping / Performance Max + Branded Search) — FOUNDATIONAL demand capture.** Not a storytelling channel, but non-negotiable to capture people already searching the brand name and category terms ("Art Deco pendant," "vintage diamond lariat"). Branded search is cheap insurance against competitors/resellers. PMax/Shopping harvests high-intent category demand. Fund this before scaling cold social if budget is tight.
- **TikTok — OPTIONAL TEST.** Repurpose Reels as Spark Ads. Can be efficient for founder-story and "antique reimagined" transformation content, but audience skews younger than the 30–60 target; treat as a small exploratory line, not a core channel, until it proves out.

## 3. Meta Campaign & Ad-Set Architecture

### 3.1 Account structure (keep it simple; consolidate for learning)
Fine-jewelry accounts at these budgets suffer from fragmentation. Run **few campaigns, few ad sets, many creatives.**

**Campaign 1 — TOF Prospecting (Awareness/Video + Traffic/Engagement)**
- Objective: Awareness (reach/video views) and/or Traffic, depending on which fills the retargeting pool most efficiently. At lean budgets, a Traffic or Engagement objective often builds a usable pool faster than pure Awareness.
- Ad sets (broad-first, then interest stacks only if broad underdelivers):
  - **Broad / Advantage+ Audience** (let Meta find buyers; 25–60, all genders initially or women-weighted). Often the top performer; give it room.
  - **Interest Stack A — Aesthetic:** Art Deco, Great Gatsby aesthetic, vintage fashion, antique collecting, "old money" / quiet luxury, 1920s–1930s design.
  - **Interest Stack B — Category/Competitor:** fine jewelry, estate jewelry, vintage engagement rings, brands like Catbird, Mejuri (aspirational tier), Jennifer Fisher, antique dealers, Sotheby's/Christie's jewelry.
  - **Interest Stack C — Occasion:** engagement, anniversary gifts, heirloom gifting, luxury gifting, bridal (Art Deco bridal is a strong niche).
- Placements: **Advantage+ Placements** (let Meta optimize), but ensure Reels + Stories + Feed are all eligible; exclude low-quality placements only after data shows waste.

**Campaign 2 — MOF / Prospecting-to-Conversion (Sales objective, broad)**
- **Advantage+ Shopping Campaign (ASC)** once pixel has signal (aim for the data threshold before relying on it). ASC blends prospecting + some retargeting and is well-suited to catalog-driven jewelry. Cap existing-customer % so it stays acquisition-weighted.
- Collection/catalog-driven creative so individual sold-out SKUs auto-drop.

**Campaign 3 — BOF Retargeting (Sales objective, manual control)**
Segmented, descending-recency, mutually exclusive audiences (exclude lower tiers from higher ones):
  - **Checkout abandoners** (last 14–30 days) — highest ROAS; dynamic "still available / 1 left" + reassurance (authenticity, returns, secure checkout).
  - **Add-to-cart, no purchase** (14–30 days).
  - **Product/collection viewers** (14–30 days).
  - **All site visitors** (30–90–180 days) — brand + social proof + new arrivals.
  - **Video viewers (50%+/ThruPlay) + IG/FB engagers** (90–365 days) — cheap, large, keeps the brand present through a long consideration window.
- Use **Dynamic Product Ads (DPA)** off the catalog for viewers/ATC so people see the exact piece again — but pair with collection-level creative so a sold piece is replaced gracefully.

**Lookalikes (use as needed, especially as pixel matures):**
- 1% LAL of **purchasers** (seed may be small early — start at 1–3%, widen as list grows).
- 1% LAL of **high-value purchasers / AOV > $1,000** once data allows (teaches Meta the premium buyer).
- 1% LAL of **ATC + checkout** and of **top 25% site dwell time**.
- Treat LALs as additional interest-style ad sets inside TOF; at scale, broad + Advantage+ Audience often beats manual LALs — test both.

### 3.2 Advantage+ Shopping vs. Manual — recommendation
- **Start manual** for TOF (Awareness/Traffic) + manual BOF retargeting while volume and pixel signal are low, so you control audiences and can read creative learnings.
- **Layer in Advantage+ Shopping** for the core sales push once the pixel has enough conversion signal. ASC simplifies prospecting-to-conversion and is efficient for catalog jewelry, but it is a black box — keep a **manual retargeting campaign running alongside it** so abandoners are always covered and so you retain readable creative data.
- At lean budget (~$1k/mo): **mostly manual + one small ASC or Sales campaign**; don't fragment. At scale: **ASC as the workhorse** + manual BOF + a TOF video-view feeder.

### 3.3 The scarcity angle for one-of-a-kind SKUs
- **Honest "only 1 available" / "1 of 1" urgency** in copy and on-image — this is *true* scarcity and it is the brand's superpower. Never fabricate countdowns or fake stock.
- **Collection-level framing:** ads sell "a rotating collection of one-of-a-kind Art Deco heirlooms — when it's gone, it's gone," so the message survives any single sell-out.
- **Catalog set up as a collection** so DPA pulls from live, in-stock pieces; set feed sync frequent enough that sold items drop within hours, not days.
- **"Just arrived / recently reimagined"** framing turns scarcity into a reason to check back — pairs with email new-arrival flows.

## 4. Pinterest Ads Plan

Pinterest is the single best *secondary* channel for this brand: pinners actively plan heirloom, bridal, and aesthetic purchases, and vintage/Art Deco is a native, high-volume interest.

**Objectives & formats**
- **Awareness/Consideration:** Standard image Pins + **Video Pins** (close-up motion of stones catching light; "antique → reimagined" transformations). **Idea/collage-style** pins for styling and the founder story.
- **Conversion:** **Shopping Ads** from the product catalog (set up the Pinterest catalog + tag) and **Collections Ads** (one hero + supporting product thumbnails — ideal for showing a curated collection vs. a single scarce piece).
- **Retargeting:** retarget site visitors and engaged pinners with Shopping/Collections ads.

**Targeting**
- **Keyword targeting:** "Art Deco jewelry," "vintage engagement ring," "antique diamond ring," "1920s jewelry," "estate jewelry," "Great Gatsby wedding," "vintage pendant necklace," "old Hollywood glamour," "heirloom jewelry," "Edwardian/Deco ring."
- **Interest targeting:** women's fashion, fine jewelry, vintage style, wedding jewelry, antiques, luxury lifestyle, "old money"/quiet-luxury aesthetic.
- **Actalike audiences** (Pinterest's lookalike) off converters/site visitors once the tag has data.
- **Age/gender:** women-weighted, 28–60.

**Creative notes for Pinterest**
- Vertical 2:3 pins, text overlay sparing and elegant, keyword-rich titles & descriptions (Pinterest is a visual search engine — the copy aids discovery).
- Pins have a long half-life; a strong organic pin can be promoted later, so coordinate paid with the organic Pinterest calendar.

## 7. Measurement & Optimization

### KPIs (and rough ⚠️ estimate ranges to replace with your own data)
| KPI | What it tells you | ⚠️ Illustrative benchmark (validate!) |
|---|---|---|
| **ROAS (in-platform)** | Platform-attributed efficiency | TOF often <1–1.5x early; BOF 3–6x+; **blended target 2–3x+** to start |
| **MER / blended ROAS** | Total revenue ÷ total ad spend — the real scoreboard | Set from contribution margin; high AOV allows lower MER than cheap-goods brands |
| **CPA (cost per purchase)** | Acquisition cost | Must stay well under AOV × margin; with $1k+ AOV, CPAs of $100–$300+ can be fine |
| **CTR (link / all)** | Creative resonance | Link CTR ~0.7–1.5%+ fine jewelry (estimate) |
| **CPM** | Reach cost / audience competition | Jewelry/luxury runs higher, esp. Q4 (estimate) |
| **Hook rate / 3s view rate** | Does the first frame stop the scroll | Aim 25–35%+ (estimate) |
| **ATC rate & cost/ATC** | Mid-funnel intent — key leading indicator at low purchase volume | Track trend, not an absolute |
| **AOV** | Mix of price ladder; watch it doesn't collapse to the $300 entry only | — |
| **Checkout abandon / recovery rate** | BOF effectiveness | — |

Because purchase volume is low, **lean on ATC rate, cost/ATC, CTR, and hook rate as early decision signals**; purchases confirm later.

### Attribution caveats
- Long, multi-day, multi-device consideration → **in-platform ROAS understates paid.** Use **blended MER** as the north star and triangulate with **post-purchase "how did you hear about us?" surveys** and Google Analytics 4 / UTMs.
- iOS/ATT and cookie loss suppress reported conversions → **deploy Conversions API** to recover signal.
- Set attribution windows deliberately (7-day click / 1-day view is common; for high-consideration you may also review **7-day click + 1-day view** vs. longer). Document the window so comparisons stay apples-to-apples.
- Expect **retargeting ROAS to look inflated** (would-have-converted buyers) — read it alongside incrementality thinking, not in isolation.

### Pixel / tag / tracking setup (do this before scaling spend)
- **Meta Pixel + Conversions API (CAPI)** — server-side via your platform (e.g. Shopify's native Meta integration or a server GTM setup). Verify events: PageView, ViewContent, AddToCart, InitiateCheckout, Purchase (with value + currency). Confirm **event match quality** and **deduplication** between pixel and CAPI.
- **Catalog/feed** connected and syncing frequently (critical for one-of-a-kind availability); map product IDs so DPA/retargeting work.
- **Pinterest Tag + Conversions API**, with the same event set, and Pinterest catalog for shopping ads.
- **Google:** Google Ads conversion tracking + GA4, Merchant Center feed, enhanced conversions.
- **UTM everything** so GA4 can see cross-channel assisted paths.
- **Consent / CMP** in place (see §9).

### Testing cadence
- **Creative:** introduce new concepts weekly; evaluate on a **rolling 7–14 day** window given low volume. Keep 1–2 "control" winners always live.
- **Audiences:** change one variable at a time; give broad/Advantage+ at least 7 days + exit of learning phase before judging.
- **Weekly review:** creative-level CTR/hook/ATC + campaign ROAS/CPA + blended MER.
- **Monthly review:** channel mix reallocation, LAL refresh, catalog audit, funnel-split rebalance.

### Kill / scale rules (⚠️ adjust thresholds to your margins)
- **Kill an ad** if, after enough spend to be meaningful (e.g. ~2–3× target CPA spent, or ~1,000+ impressions with clear underperformance), **hook rate and CTR are bottom-quartile AND cost/ATC is >2× account average** with no purchases. Don't kill on a single missing purchase alone.
- **Scale a winner:** raise budget **~20–30% every 3–4 days** (avoid resetting learning with big jumps) while ROAS/CPA holds. Duplicate proven creative into ASC/broad for incremental scale.
- **Pause an audience/campaign** if blended MER for its cohort stays below breakeven for 2+ weeks after learning phase.
- **Protect BOF:** never pause retargeting to fund a cold test.

## 8. Testing Roadmap & 90-Day Flight Plan

### What to test first (priority order)
1. **Creative concept / hook** (biggest lever): Founder Story vs. Antiques-Reimagined vs. Scarcity vs. Aesthetic — find the 1–2 that stop the scroll.
2. **Format:** Reels video vs. static/carousel vs. collection ad.
3. **Audience:** Broad / Advantage+ vs. interest stacks vs. 1% LALs.
4. **Offer/angle framing (not discounts):** scarcity ("1 of 1") vs. heritage vs. price-entry ($300 starting point) vs. gifting.
5. **Landing experience:** collection page vs. specific product vs. founder/story page as the ad destination.
6. **Channel:** Pinterest vs. Meta efficiency for the same creative.

### 90-Day flight plan

**Phase 1 — Foundation & Validation (Days 1–30)**
- Week 1: Tracking build — Pixel + CAPI, Pinterest tag + CAPI, Google/GA4, Merchant Center & Meta/Pinterest catalogs, consent/CMP, UTMs, attribution windows set. Audience assets created (visitor/VV/engager/ATC/checkout custom audiences). Branded search live (cheap insurance from day 1).
- Weeks 1–2: Launch **Meta TOF** (broad + 1–2 interest stacks) with 3–4 creative concepts × variations; launch **manual BOF retargeting**; launch **Pinterest** shopping + 1 keyword/interest set.
- Weeks 3–4: Read hook rate / CTR / cost-per-ATC. Kill bottom creatives, double down on top 1–2 concepts. Begin building purchaser list for LALs.
- *Expectation:* efficiency noisy; focus on learning + pool-building, not ROAS.

**Phase 2 — Optimize & Formalize (Days 31–60)**
- Introduce **MOF** (consideration/traffic + VV retargeting) as its own line.
- Launch **1% LALs** (purchasers, ATC) now that seeds exist; stand up **Advantage+ Shopping** if pixel signal is sufficient, running alongside manual BOF.
- Scale winning creative +20–30% every few days; refresh 2–3 new creatives/week to fight fatigue.
- Prove/disprove Pinterest; decide whether to add the small **TikTok Spark Ads** test.
- Begin reporting **blended MER** + post-purchase survey results.

**Phase 3 — Scale & Defend (Days 61–90)**
- Shift budget toward proven channel/creative combos; let **Advantage+ Shopping** carry prospecting-to-conversion with manual BOF underneath.
- Broaden winning audiences, test 2–3% LALs and new interest expansions.
- Seasonal prep: if approaching a gifting peak (holiday/Valentine's/Mother's Day/anniversary season), pre-build gifting creative and raise budgets ahead of the window; expect higher CPMs.
- Lock in a **steady-state playbook**: weekly creative refresh, monthly reallocation, quarterly concept overhaul.

## 9. Compliance & Brand-Safety Notes
- **Truthful scarcity only.** "One of a kind," "1 of 1," and "only 1 available" must be *literally true* and backed by live inventory. Never use fake countdown timers, fabricated low-stock counts, or "selling fast" claims you can't substantiate. Keep the catalog synced so you never advertise a sold piece as available.
- **No misleading claims** about gemstones, metals, carat, origin, age, or value. "Vintage," "antique," "Art Deco," "18K white gold," "diamond," "genuine," etc. must be accurate and defensible. Follow FTC Jewelry Guides for material/quality terminology (e.g. don't imply natural when treated, disclose simulated/glass stones like the "crystal & green glass" piece accurately). Any "investment," "appreciation," or "heirloom value" language must avoid implying a financial return.
- **Platform ad policies:** jewelry is allowed, but avoid personal-attribute targeting violations, respect prohibited claims, and keep landing pages consistent with ad claims (pricing, availability, returns).
- **Pricing & promotions:** if you ever run price framing, comply with reference-price/sale-pricing rules; the brand's "never discount-screamy" voice already aligns here. Shipping, taxes, and return policy must be clear and honest.
- **Tracking, consent & privacy:** implement a consent management platform for GDPR/ePrivacy (EU/UK) and CCPA/CPRA (California) — cookie/consent banner, honor opt-outs, and configure Meta/Pinterest/Google for consent mode / data-use settings. Collect and process customer data lawfully; disclose tracking in the privacy policy.
- **Image & content rights:** ensure full rights/licenses for all photography, video, music, fonts, and any "as seen in"/press logos (get written permission for publication logos and stylist/editorial credits). **UGC/testimonials:** obtain written usage rights from customers before running their content as ads; testimonials must be genuine and reflect typical experience, with disclosure if there's any incentive. Model releases for any identifiable people.
- **Founder-story accuracy:** keep the Sophia/Martin narrative factually accurate in ad copy; heritage storytelling is powerful but must stay truthful.
- **Accessibility & taste:** captions for sound-off, sufficient contrast on text overlays, and copy that stays on-brand (elegant, warm, luxury — never bargain-bin urgency).
