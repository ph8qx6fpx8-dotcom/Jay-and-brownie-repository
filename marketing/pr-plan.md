## 0. The one-paragraph strategy

Sophia Martin Jewelry sells what most jewelry brands cannot: genuine scarcity and a real story. Each piece is largely **one-of-a-kind**, built from antique and Art Deco source material and reimagined into wearable fine jewelry, and the brand carries a true founder narrative (a father named Martin, antique shows, a first silver heart pendant). PR's job is to convert that scarcity and story into **third-party credibility** — editors, trade press, and trusted creators saying it — which lowers the trust cost of a $300–$3,500 purchase from a boutique brand, drives qualified referral traffic to the site, and gives the social and email channels a steady supply of "as seen in" proof. We lead with story, not discounts, because discounting is off-brand and because one-of-a-kind inventory rewards *urgency* ("when it's gone, it's gone"), which is a gift to PR and social, not a liability.

## 1. PR objectives & how PR supports social and sales

**Primary objectives (next 12 months, set targets, then measure against Section 9):**

1. **Credibility / "as seen in" assets.** Secure `[target: 6–10]` earned placements across national lifestyle, jewelry/estate trade, and vintage/accessories press — enough to build a legitimate press bar on the homepage and a social highlight reel.
2. **Qualified referral traffic.** Convert placements and creator content into measurable sessions to product pages, tracked via UTM and referral analytics.
3. **Founder authority.** Establish Sophia Lapat as a quotable voice on Art Deco revival, antiques-reimagined / circular luxury, and heirloom collecting — so journalists return to her for trend stories.
4. **Gift-guide inclusion.** Land `[target: 3–5]` gift-guide features across the year's key windows (holiday, Valentine's, Mother's Day, wedding/anniversary).
5. **Backlinks & SEO authority.** Earn do-follow links from reputable domains to lift organic discovery for "Art Deco jewelry," "vintage fine jewelry," "one-of-a-kind heirloom jewelry."

**The PR → social → sales flywheel (how the pieces reinforce each other):**

- **Earned media → credibility.** An editor's or trade write-up is trust the brand cannot buy. This is the single biggest lever for a boutique luxury brand.
- **Credibility → social proof.** Every placement becomes an Instagram/Pexels-of-proof story highlight ("Featured in…"), a homepage press bar, and newsletter fodder. Creator content becomes reusable UGC (with rights secured).
- **Social proof → traffic.** Placements and creator posts carry tracked links; one-of-a-kind scarcity makes the CTA naturally urgent ("one available").
- **Traffic → sales + list growth.** Email capture on product pages turns PR-driven visitors who *don't* buy today into owned audience for the next drop.
- **Sales/sell-through → more PR.** "Sold in 48 hours," waitlists, and restock stories feed the next round of pitches (scarcity is itself newsworthy).

**Guardrails:** Never pitch on price or promotion. Lead with story, craftsmanship, provenance, and scarcity. Protect the luxury tone in every word — warm, heritage-driven, never salesy.

## 2. Core brand narrative & story angles

### Core narrative (the 60-second version every pitch draws from)

Sophia Lapat grew up tagging along to fine antique shows with her father, Martin — a lawyer by profession and an Americana-antiques obsessive by passion, who loved taking old objects and reimagining them into new works of art. While Martin hunted Americana, young Sophia drifted to the vintage jewelry cases; her first piece was a simple silver heart pendant. Years later she built the brand that bears her father's name, doing for antique and Art Deco jewelry what he did for Americana: giving beautiful old things a second life as wearable, modern heirlooms. Every piece is largely one-of-a-kind — history you can wear, and pass on.

Three threads run through everything: **heritage** (father-daughter, antiques, Art Deco), **scarcity** (one-of-a-kind, genuine), and **sustainability** (circular luxury — reimagining, not mining and manufacturing new).

### The six story angles

For each: the hook, the ideal outlet type, and a sample pitch headline.

**Angle 1 — The father-daughter legacy (the flagship human-interest story).**
- *Hook:* A brand named for a father, Martin, built on childhood Saturdays at antique shows. He reimagined Americana; she reimagines jewelry. The name is a tribute.
- *Ideal outlet:* National lifestyle & women's features; "how I built this"-style founder profiles; podcasts.
- *Pitch headline:* *"My Father Turned Old Things Into Art. I Named a Jewelry Brand After Him."*

**Angle 2 — Antiques reimagined / sustainable & circular luxury.**
- *Hook:* Every piece reuses antique and Art Deco material — no new mining, no new manufacturing of the core elements. Luxury with a conscience, and a counter-narrative to fast fashion jewelry.
- *Ideal outlet:* Sustainability & conscious-living press; design/interiors; eco-luxury verticals of fashion titles.
- *Pitch headline:* *"The Most Sustainable Fine Jewelry Is the Kind That Already Exists."*

**Angle 3 — The Art Deco revival trend.**
- *Hook:* Art Deco (geometry, starbursts, onyx, crystal, Deco-revival lariats) is having a renewed moment; Sophia Martin offers authentic Deco-world pieces, not mass-market "Deco-inspired" lookalikes. Founder as trend expert.
- *Ideal outlet:* Fashion/accessories editors; jewelry trade trend desks; estate-jewelry press (Rapaport's Nov–Dec 2025 issue named vintage/antique one of the fastest-growing segments — ride that wave).
- *Pitch headline:* *"Why Art Deco Jewelry Is Back — and How to Buy the Real Thing."*

**Angle 4 — The heart-pendant origin / "first piece" emotional angle.**
- *Hook:* The founder's first-ever piece was a simple silver heart pendant from an antique case — a gateway to a lifelong love. Perfect for gifting seasons and for "your first fine-jewelry piece" service stories.
- *Ideal outlet:* Gift guides; Valentine's/Mother's Day roundups; weddings/heirloom features.
- *Pitch headline:* *"Her First Piece Was a $20 Silver Heart. Now She Sells Heirlooms."*

**Angle 5 — One-of-a-kind collecting & genuine scarcity.**
- *Hook:* Not a collection you can reorder — when a piece sells, it's gone. How to collect one-of-a-kind vintage fine jewelry, and why scarcity is the new luxury.
- *Ideal outlet:* Collector & estate-jewelry press; luxury lifestyle; "quiet luxury / old money" style verticals.
- *Pitch headline:* *"There's Only One of Each: Inside the One-of-a-Kind Jewelry Collectors Are Chasing."*

**Angle 6 — Female founder building a heritage brand.**
- *Hook:* A woman founder translating a father's passion into a business; craft, curation, and connoisseurship led by a female eye. Great for founder series and Women's History Month / International Women's Day (March).
- *Ideal outlet:* Business & entrepreneurship features; women-founder series; regional business press.
- *Pitch headline:* *"She Grew Up in Antique Shows. Now She's Building a Heritage Jewelry House."*

**Angle rotation rule:** Lead with 1 and 3 for warm national/fashion targets; 2 for sustainability; 4 for seasonal/gift; 5 for trade & collectors; 6 for founder/business and March calendar hooks. Never send the same angle to two competing outlets in the same window.

## 6. Influencer & gifting strategy (social)

**Who to target — profile over follower count.** Fit matters far more than reach for a one-of-a-kind luxury brand. Prioritize creators in: **vintage & secondhand fashion**, **"old money" / quiet luxury / classic style**, **antiquing / estate-sale / flea-market content**, **bridal & "something old" / heirloom styling**, and **fine-jewelry collecting/education**. The audience should skew women ~30–60 with real disposable income.

### Tiers (set rough targets; adjust to budget)
- **Nano (1K–10K):** Highest trust and engagement; cheapest to seed; great for authentic "I found this one-of-a-kind piece" posts. Seed with gifted product. `[Target: 10–15 per quarter.]`
- **Micro (10K–50K):** Vintage-fashion and antiquing niche creators with loyal communities. Gifting + occasional modest paid collaboration for a dedicated piece. `[Target: 5–8 per quarter.]`
- **Mid (50K–250K):** Selective. Use for a hero piece or a seasonal moment; usually paid, with clear deliverables and usage rights. `[Target: 1–2 per key window.]`
- *(Macro/celebrity: out of scope initially; revisit only if a piece earns organic buzz.)*

### Seeding / gifting workflow
1. **Build a vetted list** (see tracker, Section 9): handle, tier, niche, engagement rate, audience geo/age, prior brand collabs, contact.
2. **Soft-touch DM/email first** — ask interest and sizing/style preference before shipping (respect that pieces are one-of-a-kind; don't waste unique inventory on a cold send).
3. **Curate the gift to the creator** — match the piece to their aesthetic; include a **handwritten note** referencing the father-daughter story and the piece's history, plus a **story card** (what it is, era, why it's one-of-a-kind).
4. **No strings on nano gifts**, but make it easy: provide the brand handle, 2–3 key talking points, approved hashtags, and a **tracked link / unique code** so you can measure.
5. **For paid collabs:** a short written agreement — deliverables (e.g., 1 in-feed + 2 stories), timing, **FTC disclosure required**, usage/whitelisting rights (so you can reuse the content in ads and on-site), and exclusivity window if relevant.
6. **Capture & reuse:** secure rights up front; repost as UGC, add to "as worn by" highlights, and feed the email channel.
7. **Nurture, don't transact:** re-gift favorites seasonally; the best creators become long-term brand friends.

### FTC #ad disclosure note (non-negotiable)
Any creator who receives free product or payment **must clearly and conspicuously disclose** the material connection — e.g., `#ad`, `#sponsored`, or `#gifted` — placed where it's easily seen (not buried in a hashtag block or "more" fold), in the creator's own voice, on **every** post and story. This is required by FTC guidelines and protects both the creator and the brand. Put it in writing in every brief and agreement; don't rely on verbal. `[Have counsel review the standard brief language once.]`

## 9. Measurement

**What to track:**
- **Placements:** every earned mention — outlet, date, URL, angle, tier (national/trade/regional), link type (do-follow/no-follow/none).
- **Reach / EMV:** estimated reach (circulation/UMV) and **Earned Media Value** as a directional benchmark — note EMV is an estimate, not revenue; use it to compare efforts, not to claim ROI.
- **Referral traffic:** sessions, bounce, and conversions from each placement/creator via **UTM parameters** on every link you control and **referral reports** in analytics for links you don't. Give each creator a **unique code or link**.
- **Backlinks & SEO:** new referring domains and domain authority from placements; track keyword movement for "Art Deco jewelry," "vintage fine jewelry," "one-of-a-kind heirloom jewelry."
- **Conversions / revenue influence:** last-click and assisted conversions from PR/creator sources; sell-through of featured one-of-a-kind pieces ("featured → sold in X days" is both a KPI and a future pitch).
- **Owned-audience growth:** email signups and social follows attributable to PR windows.
- **Share of voice & sentiment:** qualitative — are editors framing the brand the way we want (heritage, scarcity, sustainability)?
